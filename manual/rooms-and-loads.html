Rooms and loads

Contents

The Load calculator page (commercial: Spaces) lists every room with its loads and has the room editor. Rooms drawn on the Layout carry their area, exposure, walls, windows and doors from the plan; rooms added by hand are typed in here. Commercial zones and rooftop units are on their own page; see Commercial.

Read the room loads

What it is for. See every room's cooling and heating load and its airflow.

Where. The Load calculator page (commercial: Spaces). Both editions.

What you get.

  • The room table: Room, ft², Cool sens, Latent, Heat and CFM for each room, and a total row (with the ventilation and duct loads in the commercial edition).
  • Below it, the selected room's breakdown (BTU/h): cooling by walls, glass conduction, glass solar, doors, ceiling, floor, infiltration, people and equipment, the latent load, and heating by part. A line gives the room's glass and door area, volume and supply airflow (with its cooling and heating shares).
  • The page uses the full width in two columns, so the table and the breakdown fit on a laptop screen.

Steps. Click a room row, or Tab to it and press Enter or Space, to open it in the room editor.

How room airflow is set. A room's design airflow is the larger of its cooling share and its heating share of the blower airflow (the Manual D heating and cooling factors; ACCA Instructor's Lesson Plan pp. 34 to 35).

Add rooms

What it is for. Put the rooms in the project.

Where. The Load calculator page, the buttons above the table. Both editions.

Ways to add rooms.

  • Draw rooms on the Layout opens the Layout to trace them over the plan (see Layout: drawing the plan).
  • Add room by hand (commercial: Add space by hand) adds a room to type in.
  • Build rooms on the Plan reader builds them from a floor plan sheet (see Plans and the plan reader).
  • The Home survey adds rooms walked on site (see Home survey).
  • A BIM model's rooms can be read on the BIM models page (see BIM models and plan set to 3D).
  • Open file at the top (or a file dropped on Aerion) brings rooms in from a gbXML file from a load and energy program or a BIM program (see Files from and to other programs).

Fill exposure from plan works out each drawn room's exterior wall lengths by direction from the plan.

Delete a room. Delete room in the room editor removes it (both editions use that label). A message "Room deleted." (commercial: "Space deleted.") comes up with an Undo button that puts it back.

Edit a room

What it is for. Set what the load of a room depends on.

Where. The Load calculator page, the room editor under the room's name. A short version is in the Selection card on the Layout, with All room details to open the full one. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Name and Type (residential: Primary bed, Bedroom, Living, Family room, Kitchen, Dining, Office, Bath, Laundry, Hall / foyer, Closet, Room). The commercial edition has Occupancy type instead (see Commercial).
  2. Area from outline shows the drawn area. Area override (0 = use outline) replaces it.
  3. Ceiling: Ceiling shape (Flat or Vaulted / cathedral), Ceiling height, Above the ceiling (Vented attic, Roof deck (no attic), Conditioned space) and Floor (Slab on grade, Over vented crawl, Over garage, Over unconditioned basement, Over outdoors (cantilever), Basement slab (below grade), Conditioned space below).
  4. Construction (blank = project): pick a wall, ceiling or slab assembly for this room only, or leave it on the project's.
  5. Below-grade walls: Length of wall below grade, Depth below grade and Insulation on below-grade wall. Enter the above-grade part of these walls under Exposure. Below-grade walls add heat loss only, using the average U over the buried depth.
  6. Partitions to unconditioned space: Add partition for a wall or ceiling shared with a garage, an attic knee wall or other unconditioned space, with its area and R-value. The note under the table gives the space temperatures used, cooling / heating, and their sources: garage outdoor +5 °F / outdoor (ASHRAE Standard 152 default; ASHRAE Fundamentals ch. 17), attic the attic design temperature on Design conditions (120 °F when blank) / outdoor (ASHRAE Fundamentals ch. 17), unconditioned basement indoor / halfway (ch. 18 and 17), vented crawl halfway / 30% of the way to indoor (defaults to replace). It also gives the door U-factor used for a door into such a room, a default to replace with the door's rating.
  7. People and equipment: Occupants, and the appliances (pick one and press Add). Set house occupants to bedrooms + 1 sets the people in every room so they add up to Manual J's count.

What you get. The room's loads and breakdown change at once.

Limits. The appliance list gives each item's sensible and latent gain as a starting value. Replace it with the job's own appliances. Manual J counts the kitchen appliances as one allowance: add the "Cooking range / cooktop (Manual J kitchen)" item once per kitchen.

Add windows, doors and skylights to a room

What it is for. Put the glass and doors in the room's load.

Where. The room editor, cards Windows, Doors and Skylights. The Window, Door and Skylight tools on the Layout place them on a wall and set the facing for you. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Press Add window, Add door, Add sliding glass door or Add skylight.
  2. Fill in each row: Facing, Size (or W in and H in), Qty, Glazing, U, SHGC, Shade, Blinds / drapes, Screen, and for windows the overhang (Ovh in, Gap in).

What you get.

  • Picking a glazing type fills U and SHGC with typical values for that build (panes, low-E, argon or krypton fill, frame). Type your own numbers from the NFRC label to override.
  • Blank U, SHGC and shade use the Design conditions defaults, shown grey.
  • Size codes: 3050 is 3'0" x 5'0", 2868 is 2'8" x 6'8", or type 36x60 in inches.
  • Skylight U is raised 30% for slope and curb, as the card says.

Limits. Until a room has windows or doors, it uses the glass estimate (the default glass share of exterior wall from Design conditions). Windows added in the editor face the longest exterior wall: change the facing, or click them onto a wall with the Layout tools.

Exterior walls and exposure

What it is for. Each room's exterior wall length by direction drives its wall and glass loads.

Where. The room editor, card Exposure (Facing, Wall length ft, Glass ft²). Both editions.

What you get.

  • Rooms drawn on the plan keep their exposure up to date. When a room is deleted, moved or reshaped, the rooms next to it (and the room itself) gain or lose the exterior wall that change makes. A wall is exterior along the part of it with no other room on its other side, measured every 3 in.
  • A wall length you typed by hand is kept: only the change is added or taken off. No windows are added to a wall that has just become exterior.
  • A room drawn new with no exposure gets its walls and the default glass, the same as Fill exposure from plan.
  • A short note says which rooms changed. Undo, redo and opening a project leave the rooms as they were saved.

Limits. A room or group of rooms standing apart from the house (an ADU or a casita) gets a note in the design checks: Manual J sizes a separate building on its own.

Inside doors

What it is for. Draw the doors between rooms, for room pressure and the airflow simulation.

Where. The Layout, the Door tool on a wall two rooms share. The room editor card Inside doors lists them. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Pick Door on the Layout and click a wall shared by two rooms. An inside door goes there, the room's door width (32 in to start with) by 80 in.
  2. In the room editor, each inside door has its width, height and undercut, a Swing button and Remove.

What you get. Room pressure uses the drawn doors' widths times their undercuts as the opening under the door. The airflow simulation sends return air from a room with drawn doors through those doors only.

Doors read from the drawing. Build rooms on the Plan reader, and Read doors and pathways on the Layout for a DXF, DWG or sheet underlay, put in the doors drawn with a swing (see Plans and the plan reader).

A door into a garage or other unconditioned room. When the room on the other side is traced on the plan and named as unconditioned (garage, attic, crawl space, unconditioned basement; a porch or patio counts as outdoors), the door is in the conditioned room's loads with its partitions to that space: sensible only, the door's area times the project's exterior door U-factor times the difference to that space's temperature. Manual J (8th ed., garage partitions) and ASHRAE Fundamentals 2021 (ch. 17 and 18) work such a surface at the space's temperature. A partition wall to the same space entered by gross area has the door's area taken off. Names are matched as whole words, and not when the name says the room is lived in ("Bonus over garage", "Attic bedroom"). Such a door is not a pressure relief path, and in homes a door from a garage straight into a sleeping room is flagged (IRC 2021 R302.5.1). The door U-factor is a default to replace (see Methods, sources and defaults).

What else shows them. Inside doors (leaf and swing) are drawn on the Layout, in the 3D view, on the plan set PDF and in the drawing exports.

Limits. A door between two rooms of the house adds no load: it is inside the conditioned space. A door no longer on the room's wall (the room moved, or the other room is gone) says so and is left out.

Room pressure with the door shut

What it is for. A room that gets more supply air than its own return takes back pushes the difference out under its door. With the door shut, its pressure rises. A room whose return or exhaust takes more than its supply pulls air in under the door and drops below the rest of the house. This section shows by how much, and the transfer opening that holds the room to the target.

Where. The room editor, section Room pressure, door shut (select a room to see it). Both editions. The same numbers are in the design checks, the airflow simulation card and the permit report's Room pressure, doors shut table.

What you get.

  • The pressure with the door shut in Pa, with a plus sign (above the rest of the building) or a minus sign (below it), against the target.
  • A table: In (supply), Return, Door (the air that passes under the door), Opening (sq in), Pa and Add (1 / 2): the free area to add as one opening, and as each of the two grilles of a through-wall transfer or a jump duct.
  • A room's exhaust fans are taken out of it.

Steps.

  1. Door: leave By room type (has a door), or pick Has a door that can be shut or Open to the next room. Conference, board, meeting and training rooms count as having a door; a door word in the room's name (Theater, Wine cellar) wins over an open room type.
  2. Door width (blank = 32) and Undercut (blank = 0.75) in inches, for this room.
  3. Transfer grille or jump duct already in (sq in free) and its Transfer type: One opening (free area as entered), Through-wall transfer: two grilles, free area of each, or Jump duct: two grilles and a duct, free area of each.
  4. Project defaults: door, undercut, target: Door width, every room, Undercut, every room, Pressure target, door shut (Pa) and Discharge coefficient.

Method and sources. Flow through an opening, Q = 4005 x Cd x A x sqrt(dP), with A in ft² and dP in in. w.c. (ASHRAE Handbook, Fundamentals, ch. 16). Two grilles in series each need about 1.41 times the single opening. A jump duct's own duct adds more: size it by Manual D.

The target.

  • Homes: the ENERGY STAR room pressure target (National Rater Field Checklist Rev. 14, item 6.2 and footnote 25), for bedrooms only, with the door shut: ±3 Pa, and ±5 Pa for bedrooms with 150 cfm or more, rounded to the whole pascal. Other rooms have no target unless you enter one. It is a program target, not code: the IRC sets no pressure limit, and sizes return and transfer openings by Manual D (IRC 2021 M1602.2 item 3). Verify the checklist revision the job is rated to. The permit table says "Within ±3" or "Over ±3" under "ENERGY STAR target".
  • Commercial: no target unless you enter one under the project defaults. The pressure and the opening area are shown.

Defaults to replace. The 32 in door, the 3/4 in undercut and the discharge coefficient of 0.6 (a default for a sharp-edged opening, not a measured value). Enter the job's doors.

Code checks that go with it. In homes, the return taken from a room may not be more than its supply (IRC 2021 M1602.2 item 2): a design check and a permit table column say so. A residential return in a bathroom, closet, kitchen, garage or mechanical room is flagged (IRC 2021 M1602.2 item 4, with its kitchen and garage exceptions). Each check says to verify the edition adopted.

Limits. Loads, cfm and duct sizes do not change. Check the installed closed-door pressure in the field. Return air pathways placed on the Layout count here (see Layout: drawing the plan). Commercial plenum returns are in Commercial.

Check the worst orientation (Orientation check)

What it is for. For a plan that gets built on lots facing any direction. The house turns in steps (every exposure and the roof turn with it) and the loads run each way.

Where. The Load calculator, card Orientation check. Residential edition.

Steps.

  1. Pick Turn in steps of: 90° (4 orientations) or 45° (8 orientations).
  2. Pick Design for: The orientation as drawn or The worst case (any lot).
  3. Press Run the orientation check.

What you get. The loads in each orientation. Design for the worst case sizes the equipment from the worst orientation and gives each room the largest airflow it needs in any of them.

Option rooms

What it is for. Report a plan with and without its optional rooms (bonus room, sunroom, extended living).

Where. The Load calculator, card Option rooms. Residential edition.

Steps. Type the Option package name and tick the rooms that are options.

What you get. The check reports the house with and without them.

Room load changes

What it is for. See how the last change moved each room's loads.

Where. The Layout, Review room load changes next to Front faces, and the Room load changes panel. Both editions.

What you get. The latest before and after sensible, latent, heating and airflow values for each room that changed (Before → After (Δ change)). A known edit is labelled, including a change of the front of the house or building.

Limits. It resets when you switch projects. It does no load math of its own: it compares the room results the app already shows.

Load warnings in the design checks

These checks do not change a load. Each names the room or input and leaves the choice to you.

  • Inputs that cannot give a load. A blank outdoor temperature, an R-value of zero or a zero supply temperature rise stops the design checks, naming the input.
  • People. The rooms' people are added up and compared with the bedrooms plus one.
  • Rooms with a load and no supply outlet. Each such room is noted once, with its load, and that it is in no system's sizing. A room with no load is not mentioned.
  • A room with supply outlets from two systems is counted wholly on one of them; a warning says so.
  • Separate buildings. A room or group of rooms apart from the house gets a note.

Commercial refrigeration in a room

What it is for. Add the heat of reach-ins, prep tables, merchandisers and similar units to a room, and their price to the estimate.

Where. The room editor, People and equipment (commercial: Occupancy), the Commercial refrigeration row. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Type in the search box (reach-in, freezer, prep table, 2 door).
  2. Pick the unit, set how many, and press Add unit.

What you get. The unit goes into the room's equipment list with a sensible heat gain to the room and no latent gain, and its price goes into the estimate under Equipment. The list is the Price book's Commercial refrigeration category.

Limits. The heat is a default: from the compressor horsepower in the product name (HP x 3,800 BTU/h, rejected heat at a typical duty cycle) or, without one, from the kind of unit and its doors. Units whose condensing unit sits outside add nothing to the room. Replace the default with the nameplate or ASHRAE heat gain for the actual unit.
