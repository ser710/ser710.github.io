Shop: piece marks, patterns, hangers and cut files

Contents

The Shop page turns the duct design into what the sheet metal shop and the field crew use: piece marks and labels, flat patterns nested on sheet stock, hangers, a bound shop package and CAM cut files. The same cards also show under the duct table on the Duct sizing page. Everything here is in both editions.

Piece marks and labels

What it is for. Mark every piece from the fabrication isometrics, and print labels for them.

Where. Shop, card Piece marks. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Pick the Label stock: Avery 5163, 4 × 2 in., 10 per sheet or 4 × 6 in. thermal, one per label.
  2. Press Preview labels, then Labels (PDF). Piece list (CSV) saves the list.
  3. Use Find to look up a mark, piece or size.

What you get. A table of every piece: Mark, Qty, Piece, Size, Length, Gauge, Joint and BOD (bottom of duct). Marks read system, supply or return, then the item number on that system's iso. Each label carries a QR code with the mark, piece, size, gauge, joint, length and BOD.

Flat patterns and nesting

What it is for. Lay out the rectangular fittings flat and nest them on sheet stock, by gauge.

Where. Shop, card Flat patterns and nesting. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Pick the Stock: Sheets or Coil, cut to length. Type the Sheet width and Sheet length (in).
  2. Set Space between parts, Pittsburgh lock allowance, Button-punch flange, the Joint allowance per end for S & drive, TDC / TDF and slip, and the Square-throat elbow leg extension.
  3. Press Preview cut sheets, then Cut sheets and nesting (PDF).

What you get. The count of pieces laid out, flat parts, sheets and scrap, and a table by gauge (sheets, parts area, scrap). Rectangular straight duct, elbows, transitions and square-to-rounds are laid out flat with the allowances you set.

Limits. Round pipe, round elbows, takeoffs, boots, dampers and flex are bought and listed separately; they are not patterned. The allowances are settings: set them to your shop's own values.

Hangers and supports

What it is for. Lay out the hangers on every drawn duct and count the strap, rod, strut and anchors.

Where. Shop, card Hangers and supports. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Tick Lay out hangers. Tick Show them on the plan to see them on the Layout.
  2. Set Rectangular spacing, Round spacing, Flex spacing and From each end, at most (ft).
  3. Pick the Rectangular hanger (Straps up to the limit, trapeze above, Always strap pairs, Always trapezes) and the Strap limit (duct width) (in).
  4. Pick the Upper attachment (Beam clamp, Concrete insert, Lag screw into the framing) and the Rod size (3/8 or 1/2 in).

What you get. Hangers at each duct's size and height: within the end distance of each end, then no farther apart than the spacing. Drops run from the framing above to the top of the duct. A count of hangers, strap, rod, strut and anchors, and a table by system. They replace the strap allowance in Materials. The Code check page uses the flex spacing for its flex support item.

Limits. The spacings are settings. Set them to the SMACNA tables, the flex maker's instructions and the job's specification.

Shop package and CAM cut files

What it is for. One bound PDF for the bench, and the nested sheets as DXF files for plasma or laser nesting software.

Where. Shop, card Shop package and CAM cut files. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Press Shop package (PDF) or Preview.
  2. Press CAM cut files (DXF, ZIP).
  3. Press View cut files to see the nested sheets in the app.

What you get.

  • Shop package: a cover, a material summary with weights (straight duct by size and gauge, fittings by kind, flex by size, sheet stock and scrap by gauge), the piece list with weights, the fabrication isos with their piece tables, a detail sheet for every fitting (a picture with lettered dimensions and a table; identical fittings share one sheet), then the flat patterns and nesting. Pages are numbered.
  • CAM cut files: one DXF per nested sheet (R12, inches, origin at the sheet's lower left), with layers SHEET (stock outline, do not cut), CUT (part outlines with the seam, lock and joint allowances), BEND (fold lines) and MARK (piece marks), a cut list CSV and a README.
  • View cut files opens the sheets full screen. Pick the batch and the sheet (or step with the arrow keys), turn the layers on and off, zoom with the wheel and drag to pan. The side panel lists the parts on the sheet with their marks and sizes, the share of the sheet used and the weight. Save this DXF saves one sheet, All cut files (ZIP) the set, and Open a DXF… shows any DXF from your computer.

Limits. Kerf and lead-ins are left to the nesting software. Parts larger than the stock are listed, not nested. Round pipe and round fitting patterns are not made. During the trial, DXF and CSV exports are off.

Batches and weights

What it is for. Release the duct to the shop in batches, each with its own package and cut files, and know what each weighs.

Where. Shop, card Batches and weights. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Pick Group pieces by: System and side, System, Level, Level and system, Section, Level and section or All together.
  2. In Batch numbers by group, give each group a batch number. The same number joins groups; 0 holds a group back.
  3. With Section, set how many plan sections (1 to 9) and move a room to another section in Sections by room if you need to.
  4. Set Weight limit per batch (blank = none) (lb) to split a heavy batch.
  5. Tick Count duct wrap if the shop wraps the duct before delivery.
  6. Set each batch's status: Planned, Released, Cut, Fabricated or Delivered. Use PDF, DXF and View on a batch row for that batch alone.
  7. Batch list (CSV) and Shop list (.csv) save the lists.

What you get. Each batch with its pieces, straight footage, fittings, flex, weight and status. For all duct, the shop package starts with a batch schedule, and the CAM ZIP has one folder per batch. A batch over the weight limit is split in piece order (2A, 2B...); a single piece over the limit goes in a batch of its own and is flagged.

How weights are worked. Galvanized sheet by gauge, from the flat patterns where Aerion makes them. Stock items (round pipe, adjustable elbows, boots, start collars) use makers' published weights, fitted by size and scaled by gauge, and are marked. Flex uses boxed shipping weights by size and R-value. Duct wrap, when counted, is fiberglass at 0.75 lb/cu ft at the thickness for its R-value. Sheet metal, flex and wrap are shown separately; the limit uses the total.

Gauges over the IRC table. IRC Table M1601.1.1 stops at 20 in. and sends bigger duct to SMACNA. Those ducts take their gauge, joint and reinforcing from the app's SMACNA table at the project's pressure class, everywhere: duct sizing, isos, shop lists, nesting and weights.

Limits. Liner, hangers and reinforcing are not in the weights. The settings are kept with the project.
