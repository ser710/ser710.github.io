Ducts and duct sizing

Contents

Duct sizes come from the drawn layout. The inputs are on the Design conditions page, card Duct design. The results are on the Duct sizing page. Any single duct can override the inputs when it is selected on the Layout (see Layout: equipment, registers and ducts). Everything here is in both editions unless it says otherwise.

Set the duct design inputs

What it is for. Set the static, friction, velocity, sizes, insulation, materials and fabrication the duct sizing uses.

Where. Design conditions, card Duct design. Duct design inputs on the Duct sizing page opens it. Both editions.

Air handler. New air handlers start as picks the arrangement for new units (upflow with top supply and side return, return on a platform, filter box, closet return box, return from behind, horizontal in the attic, downflow and others). Each unit can be changed on its own on the Layout.

Static pressure.

  • Blower rated external static (in. w.c.).
  • A list of components with their pressure drop: Air filter, Supply grilles / boots, Return grille and Balancing dampers to start with. Add component adds one; × removes one.
  • The available static for the ducts is the rated static less the components.

Friction and velocity.

  • Friction rate override (0 = from Manual D) in in./100 ft. With 0, the friction rate is the Manual D figure: the available static x 100 / the longest total effective length.
  • Return TEL if no return is drawn (ft).
  • Supply trunk velocity limit, Supply branch velocity limit, Return trunk velocity limit and Return branch velocity limit (fpm).

Duct sizes.

  • Trunk size changes: At every branch (smallest trunk), Only when the trunk drops 2 in. or more, Only when the trunk drops 4 in. or more, or Never: one size the whole run (extended plenum).
  • Trunk rectangular height and Branch rectangular height (in).
  • Minimum rectangular width and Minimum rectangular height (in). Blank means no minimum.
  • Min round, supply branch, Min round, supply trunk, Min round, return branch and Min round, return trunk (in).
  • Shaped ducts (flat oval, oval, triangle and the rest) match the duct they connect to.

Duct insulation. Pick the insulation for Supply trunks, Supply branches, Return trunks and Return branches (from none, bare metal R-0, through FSK duct wrap, mineral wool, liner, duct board, elastomeric sheet and spray foam), and the R-value of Flex duct and Duct board. Duct load R-value follows these types (area-weighted) uses them for the duct loads. A table lists each type's installed R-value.

Materials. Default for trunks and Default for branches (Sheet metal, galvanized; Fiberglass duct board; Flex, fully stretched; Flex, 4% compression (typical install); Flex, 15% compression (sagging)), Flex when converting, Flag flex runs longer than (0 = off) (ft) and Avoid clashes while drawing. All branches to flex and Clear per-section material change every duct at once.

Registers and layout. Auto-place: cfm per supply register, Auto-place: distance in from the wall, Duct clearance above the ceiling (auto height), Balancing damper in every supply branch, and Size unsized registers and Resize all.

Fabrication. Rectangular transverse joint (TDC / TDF flange, S and drive slip, Standing S, Pocket lock), Rectangular section length, Round joint, Round section length, Branch takeoff off trunk (45° high-efficiency takeoff (HETO), Spin-in collar, Spin-in collar with damper, Conical spin-in, Straight tap), Balancing damper at every takeoff, and the Square → round → flex round stub and flex lengths.

Construction standard and gauges. Construction standard: IRC Table M1601.1.1 (dwelling units) or SMACNA HVAC Duct Construction Standards, and the Pressure class (gauges). The card says it: IRC gauges cover duct up to 20 in. at 1 in. w.g. or less; switch to SMACNA for larger duct, higher pressure or light commercial work. The commercial edition shows the SMACNA rectangular and round schedules.

Equipment selection. The Manual S limits (see Equipment).

Limits.

  • The velocity limits, component drops, minimum round sizes, register spacing and fabrication lengths are starting values. Replace them with your own and the job's specified figures.
  • The minimum rectangular width and height are your choice: Manual D and SMACNA do not set one.
  • Flex is sized with its friction factor, so it comes out larger than metal at the same friction rate. The factors and the fitting equivalent lengths are on the Tables page.

Read the duct sizing

What it is for. See the size, airflow, velocity and pressure loss of every duct section, and the static against the blower.

Where. The Duct sizing page, card Duct sizing. Both editions.

What you get.

  • At the top: Available static, Longest supply TEL and Longest return TEL (total effective length), Friction rate and Worst-case static.
  • Notes, for example "Primary bed: 184 CFM per register. Consider adding a register."
  • Duct sections: number, type, role, Material (which you can change on the row), CFM, Length ft, Fit ft (fitting equivalent length), Round in, Rect in, FPM, Friction /100 ft, Duct loss, Fitting loss, ΔP in.wc and Notes.
  • Supply runs (supply + worst return): each register's room, cfm, TEL, supply pressure drop, total pressure drop and whether it is within the available static.

How it is worked out. The card says it:

  • Sizes are rounded up to the next whole inch at the project friction rate, by the ASHRAE round-duct friction equation with equivalent-length fittings (the Manual D method).
  • Friction per 100 ft is for the section's material (the metal rate x the material factor). Duct loss is that rate over the section's footage. Fitting loss uses the metal rate over the fitting equivalent length.
  • Type a length to override the measured footage, or clear it to go back to measured.
  • Runs with a low total pressure drop need a balancing damper.

Minimum rectangular sizes. A rectangular duct raised to the minimum width or height gets its friction, pressure drop and velocity from the bigger rectangle (its equivalent round size). A size you typed on a duct is kept. A note says how many ducts were held to the minimum. When the width limit leaves a section too small, Aerion warns and works the losses from its actual size.

Carried sizes. A section whose bigger size is carried for fitting clearance at a takeoff says so in the Notes (see Layout: equipment, registers and ducts). The sizes, airflow and pressure drop do not change.

Limits. The designer of record checks the sizes, the static and the velocities against Manual D.

Code items that use the duct sizing (residential)

  • IRC M1601.1, ducts sized by ACCA Manual D: the check passes only when every system's static is within its rating and no duct's velocity is over its limit on the Duct sizing page.
  • Friction rate worksheet in the permit report: when you set a fixed friction rate, it is labelled "fixed rate", not ASP x 100 / TEL, and the Manual D figure for each system is shown beside it. A fixed rate above the Manual D figure is called out.
  • Flex runs longer than the limit you set are flagged in the design checks.

Fitting equivalent lengths and material factors

What it is for. Set the equivalent length of each fitting and the friction factor of each duct material.

Where. The Tables page, cards Fitting equivalent lengths and Duct material friction factors. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. In Fitting equivalent lengths, type the Branch ft and Trunk ft for each fitting (plenum takeoff, wyes, elbows, tees, the three takeoff kinds, boots and grilles, balancing damper, transitions, flex collar, reducer), with a Source / note. Branch values apply to sections serving one register; trunk values to sections serving two or more, and to returns.
  2. Add a balancing damper supply branch adds the damper's equivalent length. Turn it on only if you do not count the damper in the component losses on Design conditions.
  3. Reset fittings to starter values puts them back.
  4. In Duct material friction factors, type each material's factor (a multiplier on the galvanized metal friction rate for the same diameter and airflow).

Limits. The card says it: the fitting values are generic starter values, and the flex factors are starter numbers. Replace them with the Manual D appendix numbers for the fittings you specify, and the Manual D flex compression correction for your sizes, and note the source.

Duct schedule and duct DXF

Where. The Reports page, Export: Duct schedule (.csv), Sheet metal shop list (.csv) and Duct layout for CAD (DXF). Both editions.

What you get. The duct DXF is in feet, with SUPPLY, RETURN, ROOMS, REGISTERS and TEXT layers, ready to bring into your CAD program as a link. See Reports and letters and, for the shop, Shop.
