Reports and letters

Contents

The Reports page has the permit report, the plan set, the proposal, the scope of work letter, every export, and the cards for revisions, submittals and field tests. Export in the top bar opens it. Each PDF has a preview: the preview shows each page as it will print, and you print or download from there. Every export asks you to confirm before it saves. Print previews and exported PDFs carry a small Aerion mark in the lower-left margin of each page.

Send out (the first bar)

What it is for. The main documents on the first screen.

Where. Reports, the Send out bar at the top. Both editions; the commercial bar shows only the buttons its page has.

Buttons. Permit report (PDF), Preview permit report, Plan set (PDF), Proposal (PDF) and All exports (moves you to the Export card).

Job review

What it is for. One list of what to look at before anything goes out.

Where. Reports, Job review, beside Send out. Both editions.

What you get. Findings from the equipment, the Layout, the plan sheets, the permit checks and the scope letter, grouped by where they come from, with a count such as "6 to review · 3 notes". Each finding has a link that opens the screen it is about (Show in Layout, the Equipment page, the permit check). The export buttons stay where they are.

Limits. It reads the same flags the design pages show. It adds no check of its own.

The job summary

Where. Reports, the card with the job's name. Both editions.

What you get. The location and design temperatures, total cooling, heating, the equipment, airflow, friction rate, rooms, ventilation and total ACH, and the notes. In the residential edition it says when a permit checklist is available: turn on Title 24 (California), or pick Arizona or Idaho on Design conditions, to see one.

Plan set, permit report and other exports

Where. Reports, cards Print preview and Export. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Press Preview plan set, Preview permit report (residential) or Preview fab isos.
  2. Check the pages, then print or download from the preview.

What is in the Export card.

  • Plan set (PDF): a notations sheet with the schedules, then one plan sheet per floor with the title block, and the controls sheet when it is on.
  • Residential: Permit report: Manual J/S/D + Title 24 (PDF). It holds the loads, the Manual S section (with the maker's data at design conditions, or "rated capacity, not at design conditions"), the friction rate worksheet, the room pressure table ("Room pressure, doors shut") and the high wall units. Commercial: Commercial load report (PDF).
  • Fabrication isometrics (PDF): one 17 × 11 sheet per system, supply and return, with a dimensioned iso, item balloons and a cut list with gauges, joints and BOD.
  • Commissioning sheet (PDF) and Preview commissioning sheet.
  • Project file (.json), Room loads (.csv), Duct schedule (.csv), Sheet metal shop list (.csv).
  • Duct layout for CAD (DXF): in feet, with SUPPLY, RETURN, ROOMS, REGISTERS and TEXT layers, ready to bring into your CAD program as a link.
  • Send to the modeling program (3D) (below).

Limits. A building department decides what it accepts. The designer of record checks and signs the design. During the trial, PDFs carry a TRIAL watermark and CSV and DXF exports are off.

Hand off to another load program

What it is for. Some building departments accept Manual J, S and D reports only from a load program they already know. Design the job in Aerion, then enter it in that program quickly and exactly, and print its report for the permit.

Where. Reports, card Hand off to another load program. Both editions.

Buttons.

  • Load program hand-off (ZIP): all of it in one ZIP, with the energy model file (residential) and the room loads CSV.
  • Plan for tracing, this floor (DXF): a to-scale plan in inches, built for tracing: every room outline on its own closed polyline, the exterior walls on their own layer, each window, glass door, door and skylight at its place with a tag (W1, D1, SK1) and its size, the room name, area and ceiling, a 10 ft check bar, the north arrow and a mark that lines the floors up. The ducts are on layers you can hide.
  • Load takeoff (PDF) and Load takeoff (spreadsheet): every input behind the numbers (design conditions, constructions, infiltration, ventilation, ducts, each room's exposed walls by direction, every window and door by tag with size, U-factor, SHGC, shading and overhang, skylights, below-grade walls, partitions, people and appliances), then Aerion's room and whole-house loads.
  • Floor plans to upload (PDF) and Floor plans to upload (PNG): to-scale floor plans for programs that trace an uploaded plan. Exterior walls heavy with their lengths, overall dimensions, every opening tagged.
  • Wall-by-wall entry (PDF) and Wall-by-wall entry (spreadsheet): for programs where you type each room's walls, in the order those programs ask for it.

Steps. The card Using a load program for the permit report (step by step) explains them:

  1. Finish the design in Aerion: rooms, windows and doors drawn on the plan, constructions, design conditions and equipment.
  2. Export the floor plans to upload, or the wall-by-wall sheet, or the hand-off ZIP.
  3. In the other program, upload the plan and set its scale on the 10 ft bar or an overall dimension. Trace each room, using the room names on the plan.
  4. Enter each room from the wall-by-wall sheet, in its order.
  5. Use the same project settings as the takeoff.
  6. Copy the other program's room-by-room load table and paste it into Check against another program (below). Rooms are matched by name, and differences over the tolerance are flagged.
  7. Print the other program's report for the building department, with Aerion's plan set and duct layout beside it.

Limits. Load programs do not open another program's job file, so the job is traced or typed in once. The room names and window tags match between Aerion's plan and the sheets, so nothing has to be measured again.

Check against another program

What it is for. Compare Aerion's room loads with another program's, room by room.

Where. Reports, card Check against another program. Both editions. The residential edition also has Validation log and ACCA readiness.

Steps.

  1. Paste the other program's room-by-room load table (one room per line, with area, heating, sensible, latent and cfm), or Open report (.txt, .csv, .rtf, .pdf).
  2. Set the Tolerance (%).
  3. Read the flags, and find the input that differs.
  4. Residential: type the Other program and press Log this comparison.

What you get. Rooms matched by name, with each difference over the tolerance flagged. The validation log builds a record of how Aerion agrees with the other program across real jobs. It stays in this browser, not in the project file.

Limits. The card says it: ACCA issues its test houses only to applicants, so your jobs are the evidence until then. A match is not an approval.

Operating cost and proposal (residential)

What it is for. A proposal with up to three options side by side, their yearly energy cost and payback.

Where. Reports, card Operating cost and proposal. Residential edition. (The commercial edition has the Annual energy page; see Commercial.)

Steps.

  1. Pick the Method: 8,760-hour simulation or Degree days (quick).
  2. Set the Electric rate and Gas rate, or press State average rates. Check Heating degree days and Cooling degree days.
  3. Pick the Heating fuel: Heat pump, Gas furnace or Electric strip.
  4. Fill in up to three options: install price, SEER2, HSPF2, AFUE and description.
  5. Edit Included, Not included and Terms. These are the same text the scope of work letter uses.
  6. Press Preview proposal, then Proposal (PDF). Preview rebate data sheet and Rebate data sheet (PDF) make the rebate sheet.

What you get. Each option's energy per year and cost per year. The proposal prints them side by side with payback against the Good option, the labor level the estimate is at (both prices when the estimate is priced), and the Good / Better / Best prices sent from Materials. The rebate sheet lists the data the state and utility programs ask for.

How the 8,760-hour run works. The card says it: a synthetic year fitted to the weather station's normals (the card shows them), the design loads hour by hour, equipment capacity and efficiency against outdoor temperature from the ratings, cycling, defrost and strip heat.

Limits. The card says it: operating cost is a planning estimate, not a utility bill forecast. State average rates are averages, not your utility's rates. Rebate programs vary by state; the card notes that the federal heat pump tax credit ended for equipment placed in service after December 31, 2025.

Scope of work letter

What it is for. A letter written from the project: who it is for and from, the basis of design, the equipment, the ductwork, ventilation and exhaust, controls, start-up and the documents handed over, then what is and is not included, the terms and the acceptance lines.

Where. Reports, card Scope of work letter. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Read and edit the letter in the box. Lines starting with # are headings and lines starting with - are list items in the PDF.
  2. Press Preview, Save as PDF or Copy text.
  3. Press Rebuild from the design to write it again from the project after a change. Undo brings the edited letter back.
  4. With Aerion AI on, Improve the wording has the AI rewrite it in plain, professional wording (see Aerion AI).

What you get. Every number in the letter is the project's own: each system's kind, size, airflow, rooms and model; high wall units, outdoor units and line sets; feet of supply and return duct by material, registers and grilles, where the ducts run and their insulation, balancing dampers, the transfer openings room pressure calls for, the wall openings and floor penetrations with their sizes; and the labor level. An edited letter is kept with the project. When the design changes after the letter was made, the card says so.

Limits. The Included, Not included and Terms text is your own wording to edit.

Revisions and change orders

What it is for. Issue a revision each time the drawings go out, then compare the design now with any revision to cloud the changes, list them and price the change order.

Where. Reports, card Revisions and change orders. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Type a Description (for example "Issued for construction") and press Issue Rev 0. The message says the revision was issued and that changes from then on are compared with it.
  2. Tick Revision block on the sheets and Cloud the latest changes on the plan set to show them on the plan set.

Submittal package

What it is for. A submittal for the engineer, general contractor or building department.

Where. Reports, card Submittal package. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Fill in Submittal # and Submitted to.
  2. Tick Include the load summary if you want it.
  3. For each item in the table, use Attach PDF or photo to add the maker's cut sheet.
  4. Press Preview front pages, then Submittal package (PDF).

What you get. A cover, an index, the equipment list, the AHRI match and the load summary, then each item's cut sheet stamped with its item number. The card counts the cut sheets attached.

Limits. Attachments stay in this browser.

Field tests and compliance (residential)

What it is for. Enter the test results as the tests are done, compare them with the energy code targets, and print the test report and the energy certificate for the inspector.

Where. Reports, card Field tests and compliance. Residential edition.

Steps.

  1. Pick Targets from: automatic (from the design city), IECC 2018, 2021 or 2024, the Florida Building Code, Energy Conservation (8th edition, 2023), or California Title 24, Part 6. The card names the tester the code allows.
  2. Fill in the Tester (name, company, license or certification number, issued by, test date, permit number).
  3. Enter the results: Envelope (blower door) airflow at 50 Pa (the conditioned volume comes from the rooms unless you type it); Ventilation measured airflow; and for each system the duct test stage, total leakage and leakage to outside (cfm25), measured airflow, blower watts, total external static and the charge check (subcooling, superheat or weigh-in, target and measured).
  4. Fill in For the certificate: the water heater and its efficiency.
  5. Press Preview test report, Field test report (PDF), Preview energy certificate or Energy certificate, IECC R401.3 (PDF).

What you get. A Results table with each test, its target, the measured value and a status. The certificate goes on the wall at the furnace or air handler, or in the utility room; it lists insulation, windows, the leakage results and the equipment efficiencies. Duct and envelope tests follow ANSI/RESNET/ICC 380, as the card says.

Energy code software (residential)

What it is for. Send the house to the energy code program instead of typing it again.

Where. Reports, card Energy code software. Residential edition.

Steps. Press HPXML file, or Preview input summary and Input summary (PDF).

What you get. HPXML is the standard home energy file those programs import. The input summary lists the same values for typing in by hand. The card lists what is missing (for example the climate zone, or SEER2 and HSPF2 from the AHRI match).

Limits. Check the import against the summary. In Florida, the card says, the run is Form R405 (performance) or R402 in Commission-approved software.

Send to the modeling program (3D)

What it is for. Build the duct design as real elements in the BIM program, through the Aerion add-in.

Where. Reports, Export, Send to the modeling program (3D). Both editions. Plan set to 3D on the BIM models page has its own Send to the modeling program.

Steps.

  1. Pick Build it in: A new model, from a template, or A copy of my model (your file is not changed) with My model (full path of the .rvt).
  2. Type the New file name and pick the Version.
  3. Send it. The job goes to Documents\Aerion\Jobs.
  4. In the BIM program, press Run jobs on the Aerion tab.

What you get. Ducts at their sizes and heights (round and rectangular sheet metal, flex as flex duct, risers, and a vertical piece wherever ducts at different heights meet), supply and return on their own systems; an elbow at each bend, a transition at each size change, a takeoff where a branch leaves a straight trunk, a tee or cross where runs meet; each register and grille as an air terminal with a boot up to the duct; the air handlers as their cabinet, coil, plenums and pan. Tags, airflow and Aerion's duct ids go into Mark and Comments. In your own model, levels are matched by height, never renamed or removed.

Limits. It needs the Aerion add-in in the BIM program (see BIM models and plan set to 3D). Duct types and fittings come from the template's routing preferences.
