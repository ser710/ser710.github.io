Controls and schedules

Contents

The Controls page sets up each system's thermostat, zoning, control wiring and power circuits, and draws the wiring diagram for the plan set. The Schedules page holds the title block and the equipment schedules that print on the plan set. Both pages are in both editions.

Set up a system's controls

What it is for. Pick the control type, set the electric heat and see the thermostat terminals.

Where. Controls, one card per system (for example "FAU-1 · Main floor" or "RTU-1 · Conference and training"), section Controls. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Pick the Control type: an automatic choice, Conventional 24 V, Communicating (A B C D) or Ductless (outdoor to head). The commercial edition also has Cooling stages (Automatic, Single stage, Two stage, Variable speed).
  2. Type the Electric heat (kW) if it differs from the default shown.

What you get. The thermostat and outdoor unit terminals for that control type, for example A · B · C · D for communicating controls, or R · C · G · Y1 · O/B · W1 for a conventional heat pump.

Zone a system

What it is for. Split a system into zones, each with its own thermostat and dampers.

Where. Controls, the system's card, section Zoning, Zoned. On the Layout, the Zones tool moves rooms between zones (see Layout: equipment, registers and ducts). Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Tick Zoned.
  2. Set the zones in the card, or pick Zones on the Layout and click rooms into each zone.

What you get. Dampers, thermostats and the excess-air check update as you go. Tint zones on the plan shows the zones on the Layout.

Size the power circuits

What it is for. Work out the breaker and wire for each unit, and where the disconnects go.

Where. Controls, the system's card, section Power. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Type the MCA and MOCP from each nameplate (outdoor unit, air handler, or package unit).
  2. Place the electrical panel on the plan: Set the panel on the plan here, or the Panel tool on the Layout. That measures the power runs.

What you get. For each unit, the breaker (poles and amps) and the copper wire size, with the disconnect and service receptacle notes and their NEC sections (for example a disconnect within sight, NEC 440.14; a service receptacle within 25 ft, NEC 210.63). Values from the spec sheet are marked † (confirm on the nameplate); values estimated from the unit's size are marked * (enter the nameplate).

Limits. The breaker and wire sizes follow from the MCA and MOCP. An estimated MCA and MOCP is only a placeholder: enter the nameplate values. The electrician and the electrical drawings govern.

Read the wiring diagram and control runs

Where. Controls, the system's card, Wiring diagram, and the Control wiring runs card. Both editions.

What you get.

  • A diagram from the thermostat to the air handler (or package unit) and the outdoor unit, with the cable for each run (for example 18/4 thermostat cable) and its length once the thermostat is placed, and the power runs from the panel.
  • Control wiring runs: each cable, from, to, run length and what it serves. Run lengths follow the plan at right angles, plus the rise or drop and 10 ft for service loops, as the card says.

Limits. The card says it: colors are typical; the equipment wiring diagrams govern. 18 AWG Class 2 control wiring (NEC 725) runs apart from line voltage.

Put the controls on the plan set

Where. Controls, card Controls and wiring. Both editions.

Steps.

  • Place thermostats puts a thermostat for each system on the plan.
  • Preview controls sheet shows the controls sheet.
  • Tick Controls sheet in the plan set, Show wiring runs on the plan and Tint zones on the plan as you want them.

What you get. The diagrams print on a controls sheet in the plan set, and the wire, dampers and breakers go into the bill of materials.

Fill in the title block

What it is for. Set what prints in the title block of every plan sheet.

Where. Schedules, card Title block. The company fields are also in Options, Company and title block. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Type the Job #, Performed by, For (client), Job address and City, state.
  2. Type the Company, Company address, City, state, ZIP, Phone and Email.
  3. Pick the Sheet size (30 × 42 (E1), 24 × 36 (ARCH D), 18 × 24 (ARCH C), 11 × 17 (tabloid)) and the Plan scale (1/8", 3/16" or 1/4" = 1'-0", or Fit to sheet).
  4. Press Export plan set (PDF), or export it from the Reports page.

What you get. The plan set prints a notations sheet with the schedules, then one plan sheet per floor with the title block.

Fill the equipment schedules

What it is for. The outdoor unit, indoor unit, controller and HRV / ERV schedules on the plan set.

Where. Schedules, card Schedules and the schedule cards. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Press Fill outdoor and indoor schedules from the design. It builds one row per air handler and one per outdoor unit from the Equipment selections, with the airflow and ESP from the duct design. It replaces the outdoor and indoor rows.
  2. Type what the submittal gives: electrical (MOCP, MCA, volt-phase-hertz), weight and refrigerant.
  3. Use Add row for the Controller schedule and the HRV / ERV schedule, and for any row you add by hand.
  4. Add Schedule notes (one per line) under each schedule.

What you get.

  • Outdoor unit schedule: tag, area served, manufacturer, model, AHRI ref #, modules, nominal tons, MOCP, MCA, volt-phase-hertz, weight, EER, COP, added refrigerant, indoor units served, notes.
  • Indoor unit schedule: tag, area served, manufacturer, model, airflow, ESP, MOCP, MCA, volt-phase-hertz, weight, notes.
  • Controller schedule and HRV / ERV schedule.

Ventilation note and general notes

Where. Schedules, cards Whole house mechanical ventilation (commercial: whole building) and Mechanical general notes. Both editions.

What you get.

  • The ventilation calculation as it prints, for example "ASHRAE 62.2 compliance calculation: 1,344 sq. ft. conditioned floor area × 0.03 + 7.5 × (3 bedrooms + 1) = 70 CFM minimum continuous outside air required", with an Extra line you can type and Print 62.2 prescriptive duct sizing table.
  • Mechanical general notes: one note per line. They print numbered. Edit them for the job.

Limits. The general notes are a starting text. The designer of record is responsible for what they say.
