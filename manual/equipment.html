Equipment

Contents

The Equipment page compares equipment with each system's load: the size, the maker's capacity at the design conditions, airflow, heating and the blower check. The air filter database is on the same page. The residential checks follow ACCA Manual S. The commercial edition's rooftop units have their own checks (see Commercial).

Pick the equipment size for a system

What it is for. Compare nominal sizes with the system's load and pick one.

Where. The Equipment page, cards Equipment inputs and Equipment selection. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. In Equipment inputs, pick the System: each air handler or unit, or Whole house (the whole load on one unit).
  2. Pick the system type in the second System list (residential: Heat pump (strip backup), AC + gas furnace, AC + electric strip; the commercial list has no gas furnace).
  3. Set Capacity at design conditions (% of nominal) and Equipment sensible ratio if you know them.
  4. Set Airflow per ton and, if you need it, Airflow override (0 = auto).
  5. Leave Nominal size on Automatic, or pick a size.

What you get. Equipment selection shows the Total load, the size Selected, the System airflow and the Heating load, and a table of each nominal size with its total BTU/h, % of load, sensible, latent and a verdict (Undersized, In window, Oversized). On the ranch sample, picking 3 ton changes Selected to 3 ton and System airflow to 1,200 cfm. Below the table: Heating (the electric heat needed and the nearest standard strip) and Airflow (supply cfm at the cfm per ton, the heating airflow at the supply rise, and the duct static against the rating).

Limits.

  • Without the maker's expanded data, the capacities are nominal (nominal x the capacity factor), and the sensible and latent checks ask for the maker's data instead of passing.
  • When a job has several systems, a note says the Whole house size (one unit for the whole load) is not the systems' size.
  • The designer of record checks the selection against ACCA Manual S.

The Manual S size limits the checks use

What it is for. The maximum size follows the compressor type of the outdoor unit you picked and the Manual S edition the job's IRC references, instead of one flat limit.

Where. Design conditions, card Duct design, section Equipment selection: Manual S limits (By equipment type and code edition or Fixed maximum (below)), the heating-dominated box, and Fixed maximum cooling capacity (% of load). The limits show in the Equipment page, the Code check page and the permit checklist. Residential edition.

The limits.

Job's IRCManual S editionSingle-speedTwo-speedVariable-capacity
2018 or 2021Manual S (2014)90% to 115%90% to 120%90% to 130%
2024Manual S-2023 with proposed Addendum c120% at a load of 24,000 BTU/h or less, else 115%125%130%
  • Minimums for every type: 90% of the total load, 90% of the sensible load, 100% of the latent load.
  • Manual S (2014), heat pumps: up to the cooling load + 15,000 BTU/h when the home's Manual J sensible heat ratio is 0.95 or more and the climate is heating-dominated (HDD65 / CDD50 of 2.0 or more). The app has no degree days, so tick Heating-dominated climate yourself.
  • Manual S-2023, dry climate (sensible heat ratio 0.95 or more): single-speed up to the cooling load + 6,000 BTU/h; for two-speed, the minimum-speed capacity 115% of the load or less. The app has no low-speed capacity, so the check asks for the maker's figure.
  • With no outdoor unit picked, the type is not known: the single-speed limit is shown, and the check says so.

Sources. ACCA HVAC Blog, L. Escobar, "Evolving Industry, Revised Standard", May 2014; ACCA Proposed Addendum c to Manual S-2023 (2025), Tables N2.3.1, N2.3.2 and N2.3.4 (labelled "2023 + proposed Addendum c" in the app, because it is proposed); docs/load-calc-sources.md item 10.

Limits. Fixed maximum holds every unit to one limit you type. The Manual S edition goes by the IRC the permit jurisdiction adopted; confirm it.

Load a certified model

What it is for. Put a real outdoor unit and indoor unit on the system.

Where. Equipment inputs, cards Outdoor unit and Indoor unit. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. In Outdoor unit, pick Brand, Type (Heat pump, AC condenser, Ductless outdoor unit), Series and Size.
  2. Read the Spec sheet lines: model, cabinet size, weight, MCA / MOCP, sound power, line sizes, factory charge, maximum line length and lift, and ratings.
  3. Press Use model … (the button names the model).
  4. Do the same in Indoor unit (Air handler / fan coil, or Gas furnace).

What you get. The model numbers go into the model fields. Blank nameplate fields in the app use the spec sheet values. Picking a ducted outdoor unit sets the tonnage, sensible ratio and airflow. The compressor type of the outdoor unit sets the Manual S limit.

Limits. The catalog lists product lines with nominal sizes and typical efficiency. Enter the AHRI matched model number and use the maker's expanded data for the final Manual S check.

Find a certified match in the ENERGY STAR directory

Where. Equipment inputs, ENERGY STAR heat pump directory. Both editions.

Steps. Type a Brand or Model contains, set Tons, pick the type (Any type, Ducted split, Mini / multi split, Packaged), set SEER2 ≥, tick Cold climate if you need it, and press Search. Load a match from the list.

What you get. The AHRI reference, outdoor and indoor models, SEER2, EER2, HSPF2 and capacity at 95, 47, 17 and 5°F.

Limits. Air conditioners are no longer ENERGY STAR certified, so this list is heat pumps only, as the card says.

Read an AHRI certificate

Where. Equipment inputs, cards AHRI certificate and AHRI match (type or edit). Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Press Open AHRI Directory, open the certificate, select all of its text and copy it.
  2. Paste it in the box and press Read certificate. Or type the fields in AHRI match: AHRI ref #, Brand, the outdoor, indoor and furnace models, Cooling capacity A2 (BTU/h), SEER2, EER2, HSPF2, Heating at 47°F, Heating at 17°F, Furnace AFUE (%) and Certificate date.
  3. Or pick from Rated data on file (from manufacturer submittals) and press Use these rated points. That fills the AHRI fields and the expanded performance table with the submittal's rated points; the AHRI reference number still comes from the certificate.
  4. Print AHRI summary (all systems) and Preview print the matches.

Limits. The AHRI Directory has no public lookup a page can call, so the app cannot fetch a certificate from its number alone.

Enter the maker's expanded performance data

What it is for. Check Manual S at the job's design conditions with the maker's own table, instead of the rated capacity.

Where. Equipment inputs, card Manufacturer expanded performance. Both editions (the residential checks use it as below; commercial rooftop units keep their own checks).

Steps.

  1. Tick Use these numbers for the Manual S check.
  2. Leave Design entering wet bulb (blank = from indoor design) and Design airflow (blank = system cfm) blank, or type them.
  3. Type the cooling rows straight from the maker's table: Outdoor °F, Entering WB °F, Indoor DB °F, CFM, Total BTU/h, Sensible BTU/h. Add row adds one.
  4. For a heat pump, type the heating rows: Outdoor °F, Heating BTU/h (heat pump), CFM.
  5. Type the Compressor lockout (°F and colder) and its Lockout source (the equipment document and the control setting). Leave it blank if it is not verified.
  6. Or paste: copy the rows from a spreadsheet (or a PDF table pasted into one), with or without the header row, into Paste from a spreadsheet. Pick Rows are Cooling or Heating (heat pump), tick or clear Replace the rows now in the table, and press Add pasted rows. Undo puts the table back.
  7. Equipment database, Import expanded performance takes a maker's table as CSV or Excel for every project in this browser.

What you get. The card Manual S, maker's expanded data at design conditions shows the capacity at the design conditions with the rows and the interpolation used:

  • Outdoor dry bulb from the cooling design temperature; indoor dry bulb from the indoor design; entering wet bulb worked from the indoor design temperature and humidity at the job's elevation (ASHRAE Handbook, Fundamentals 2021, ch. 1 psychrometrics), unless you type one; airflow from the system's design airflow, unless you type one.
  • Straight-line values between the printed rows on each axis (airflow, entering wet bulb, indoor dry bulb, outdoor). Latent is total minus sensible.
  • A value outside the table is flagged, and no capacity is given: the app never reads past the ends of the table.
  • Where the table has only one value on an axis and the design differs, the numbers are marked "not at design", for information only.
  • With all published heating airflows kept, the heating capacity uses the selected airflow when the table has airflow.
  • The same section prints in the permit report. With no data, both say "rated capacity, not at design conditions".

Sources. ACCA Manual S-2023, N1.7 and N1.12 (capacity from the maker's data at the design operating conditions); DOE Building America Solution Center on interpolating and not extrapolating heat pump capacity.

Limits. The entering air is taken as room air: no return duct gain or outdoor air mixing on the entering wet bulb. That is a default to replace: type the design entering wet bulb when return-side gains or ventilation mixing change it. Replacing a model clears its lockout and source.

Heat pump balance point and supplemental heat

What it is for. See where a heat pump stops covering the load and how much backup heat it needs at design.

Where. The Equipment page, Heating, and the permit report's Manual S section, once heating rows are entered. Residential edition.

What you get.

  • The heat pump's heating capacity at the winter design temperature, read from the maker's rows.
  • The thermal balance point: where the maker's capacity line crosses the heating load line.
  • The supplemental heat at design: the design heat loss minus the heat pump's capacity there, never below zero, in kW (1 kW = 3,412.14 BTU/h). Furnace backup is shown in BTU/h.
  • At or below the compressor lockout, or when the cutoff for expanded heating data is not verified, the compressor gets no credit and the backup covers the full design load. A blank lockout source keeps the cutoff unverified.

Method and defaults. The load line is straight in outdoor temperature, from the Manual J design heat loss at the outdoor design temperature down to zero at 65°F. The 65°F is a default to replace when the project has a known balance temperature; it is not the indoor setpoint. Between the maker's points, values are straight-line; outside the table no capacity is given.

Limits. Without heating rows the card says "The balance point is not evaluated here." Check the result against Manual S and the maker's data.

Design airflow against equipment airflow

What it is for. Make sure the ducts are sized for the blower's airflow.

Where. The Code check page and the permit checklist, one item per system. Residential edition.

What you get. The duct design cfm (the sum of the room supplies) against the equipment airflow (tons x cfm per ton). A difference over 10% is flagged. Cfm per ton outside 350 to 450 is noted as outside the usual range, to check against the maker's blower data.

Limits. The 10% is this app's default to replace, not an ACCA figure.

Derate the outdoor unit for the site

Where. Equipment inputs, Outdoor unit derating, Derate the outdoor unit for site conditions. Both editions.

What it is for. Take the site's conditions off the outdoor unit's capacity. Use the maker's data for the derate where you have it.

Import your own equipment data

Where. The Equipment page, card Equipment database: Import AHRI matches and Import expanded performance. Both editions.

What you get. An AHRI Directory export (or any list of certified matches) and the makers' expanded performance tables, as CSV or Excel. They stay in this browser for every project.

Check the air filter pressure drop

What it is for. Find the pressure drop of an air filter at your airflow and size, and check it against the blower's static.

Where. The Equipment page, card Air filter database. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. In Airflow from, pick a system, or Typed airflow and type the Airflow in cfm.
  2. Pick Size (or Auto for each filter), How many, and Design for: Clean (initial), Mid-life (halfway to change-out) or At change-out.
  3. Filter the list by Type, MERV, Show (All filters, Residential, Commercial) and Sort by, or Search.
  4. Press Curve on up to eight filters to chart them in Pressure drop vs airflow, with a table of their drops every 100 cfm.
  5. This table (CSV), Preview report and Report (PDF) save the list.

What you get. Each filter's drop, read from that filter's own published data at your airflow and size. With Auto for each filter, each filter is shown at the smallest size that keeps its face velocity at or under 300 fpm (1 in.), 350 fpm (2 in.) or 450 fpm (deeper), as the card says. † marks a value outside the published data, extrapolated. The Sources and method card lists every source.

Method. Between published points, the drop follows a straight line on log-log axes; beyond them it follows the power law through the nearest two points. A filter with one published point is scaled with the median exponent, as the Sources and method card explains.

Add your own filter. In Add a filter, type the Name, MERV, Depth, whether the Points are face velocity (fpm) or airflow through one size (cfm), the Size if cfm, the Points (velocity or cfm : drop) such as 300:0.12, 500:0.24, and the Final resistance. Press Add the filter. It is saved with this project.

Limits. The face velocity sizing rule is the app's way to pick a size for the comparison; use the filter maker's rated airflow for the final pick.

Commercial and hydronic equipment (commercial)

Where. The Equipment page, card Commercial and hydronic equipment. Commercial edition only.

What it is for. Pick a packaged rooftop unit, VRF outdoor unit, air handler (CHW coil), air-cooled or water-cooled chiller, or condensing boiler from a generic catalog, sized against the system's load.

Steps. Press the type, set Min capacity and Max capacity (x load), then Select on the one you want.

Limits. The card says it: the catalog is generic, at standard rating conditions. Confirm the final pick with the maker's data. Rooftop units, their catalog and their checks are on the Commercial loads page; see Commercial.
