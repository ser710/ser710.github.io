Snip part of a plan sheet

What it is for. Cut out part of a sheet as a picture: to paste into an email or another program, to save as a PNG file, or to paste onto a sheet as a picture markup.

Where. The Plans & markup page, the Snip button. The same tool is in the markup tools as Snip (snipping tool). Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Open Plans & markup and open a sheet. Snip stays off until a sheet is open.
  2. Press Snip.
  3. Drag a box over the part of the sheet you want.
  4. The Snip window opens with the picture. Pick one:
    • Copy image: puts the picture on the clipboard, to paste into an email or another program.
    • Save PNG: saves the picture as a PNG file.
    • Paste here: puts the picture on the sheet as a picture markup.

What you get. A picture of the part of the sheet inside the box.

Limits.

  • It takes what is drawn on the sheet on screen; it does not read the sheet's text or sizes.
  • Copy image does not work at present: the message says the system clipboard refused the image. Use Save PNG and attach the file, or Paste here to put the picture on a sheet.

How the plan reader picks out duct sizes

What it is for. The Plan reader page reads a plan set and lists the HVAC and mechanical callouts it finds. A size like 14x8 is only listed as a duct when it reads as a duct size.

Where. The Plan reader page. Both editions. There is nothing to set: this is how the reader works.

What it leaves out. A "number x number" is not taken as a duct when:

  • it has a third part, as steel does (6x6x1/4);
  • it follows a steel or lumber mark (HSS, LVL, GLB, DF, PT, DBL and the like);
  • it sits next to a framing, structure or opening word (post, column, beam, header, joist, rafter, footing, pier, @ 16" O.C., window, door, hatch, tile and the like);
  • it is followed by cm, mm or ft, or by a room or floor-finish word;
  • it is on a structural sheet.

A duct word next to the size keeps it in (duct, supply, return, SA, RA, CFM, trunk, register, up, down and the like), so "14x8 return in joist bay" is still read as a duct.

On a sheet that is not mechanical (an A, S or I sheet, for example), a size needs a duct word next to it. On a sheet with no sheet number, a bare size counts only when the sheet has other HVAC callouts.

Limits. The reader goes by the words printed next to a size. A duct size with no duct word next to it, on a sheet that is not mechanical, is left out. Check the list against the drawings.
