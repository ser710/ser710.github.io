Start: the screen, projects and samples

Contents

This page covers what you see when Aerion opens, how to start a project, and how projects are kept. Everything on this page is in both editions unless a section says otherwise.

What Aerion is for

What it is for. Aerion is an HVAC design app. It works out room-by-room loads, compares equipment with them, sizes the ducts, draws the duct layout, prices the job and prints the permit and hand-off documents. The residential edition follows the structure of ACCA Manual J, S and D. The commercial edition (Aerion Commercial) works hourly loads by the ASHRAE radiant time series method and outdoor air by the ASHRAE 62.1 ventilation rate procedure.

Limits. Aerion does the arithmetic. It is a design aid. The designer of record checks the loads, the equipment, the ducts and the code items before anything is built, priced or sent for a permit. The residential Design conditions page says it plainly: the app is not an ACCA-approved program, so a permit submittal that needs one still needs one. ACCA approval is in progress; the Manual J8 page tracks it (see Code check, energy code and Manual J8).

The screen

Where. Every page. Both editions.

The window has three parts:

  • The page list down the side. The pages are in groups: Overview, Design, Documents, Field and Settings. On a laptop or desktop you can close a group you do not use. Aerion remembers the groups you closed in that browser.
  • The top bar, with New, Open, Save, AI (Aerion AI), Ductulator (a duct calculator), Pop out (this page in its own window, for a second monitor) and Export (opens the Reports page).
  • The page itself. Most pages are made of cards. A card has a title, its fields and its own buttons.

The pages, in the order of the list:

Keyboard. In the page list, the arrow keys move between pages, Home and End jump to the first and last page, and Enter or Space opens the page and moves you into it. On the Load calculator, Tab reaches the room rows, and Enter or Space picks a room.

Floating panels. Many cards have a small button in the corner, Float this panel (drag it anywhere, or pop it out to a window). Use it to keep a card in view while you work on another page.

Messages name the pages the way the page list does (Load calculator or Spaces, Design conditions, Reports).

How much you see (experience level)

What it is for. Show more help or less. The calculation is the same at every level; only the screens change.

Where. The Dashboard, card How much do you want to see?. The same card is on Options. Both editions.

Steps. Pick one:

  • Guided: for someone new to the design manuals or to the app. It shows what to do next and keeps the screens simple.
  • Designer: everything, with help on the terms.
  • Expert: everything, no help text.

What you get. The choice is kept in this browser. The commercial edition words the levels for commercial work (ASHRAE RTS, 62.1 and SMACNA).

Start a new project

What it is for. Open a clean project for a new job.

Where. New in the top bar, or New project on the Projects page. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Press New. The New project window opens.
  2. Type the Job name.
  3. Type the Project ZIP code (optional).
  4. Pick Start with: Design conditions, Import a plan set or Blank layout.
  5. Press Create project. Or press Open a sample to load a sample job instead, or Cancel.

What you get. A new project, open on the page you picked. A ZIP code fills in the location and climate data the lookup can find. The Design conditions page says which weather preset or matched station the project started from.

Limits. The window says it: confirm the design conditions before sizing. The ZIP lookup needs internet.

Practice on a sample job

What it is for. Learn the app on a job that is already drawn, loaded and sized.

Where. The Projects page, or Open a sample in the New project window.

The samples.

  • Residential: Ranch sample (a 48 x 28 ft ranch on one floor, seven rooms), Two-story sample, and Atherton custom home (a large custom home with several systems).
  • Commercial: a one-story office building (6,400 ft², four rooftop units) and a three-story office building.

Limits. The samples are for learning. Their inputs are not design values for a real job.

Keep, open and share project files

What it is for. Keep your work and move it between computers.

Where. The Projects page and the top bar. Both editions.

What the Projects page shows. How many projects are in this browser and how much of the browser's storage they use. Each project shows its cooling tons, heating MBH and airflow, with Open, Duplicate and Delete. The active project has Dashboard, Duplicate, Save file and Delete.

Steps.

  1. Fill in Active project details: Project name, Client, Job address and Designer. These feed the plan set title block and the reports. More title block fields are on the Schedules page.
  2. Press Save in the top bar, or Save file on the project, to keep a project file.
  3. Press Open in the top bar, or Import file on the Projects page, to open a file.

What you get. The project is kept in this browser as you work. A saved file is the copy to keep, back up and send to someone else.

Limits.

  • Browser storage is small, and the page shows how much is used. Save a file for every job you want to keep.
  • In the desktop app, Save and Open use the normal Windows file windows, and an Aerion project file opens in Aerion when you double-click it.
  • To share files with your own team, see Business, clients and team.
  • To save on a timer or by hand instead of on every change, to keep a project file in a folder by itself, or to keep backup copies, see Saving.

The Dashboard

What it is for. See the job at a glance.

Where. The Dashboard page. Both editions.

What you get.

  • The job's name and location, its rooms, floor area, floors and supply registers.
  • Total cooling (tons and BTU/h), Sensible / latent with the sensible heat ratio, Heating (MBH), Supply airflow (cfm), Ventilation (cfm) and Duct static (in. w.c.) against the blower rating.
  • Glass solar load by hour (residential): the glass load across the July design day, its hourly average and the 130% line of the Manual J exposure diversity (AED) check. The block load uses the hourly average.
  • Cooling load breakdown: the share of each part of the cooling load.
  • Room airflow: the cfm for each room.
  • Design checks: floor area per ton, supply airflow density, sensible heat ratio, duct velocity limits, duct static against the blower rating, the equipment sizing window and glass exposure diversity.
  • Recent calculations: exports, saves and Log snapshot are recorded here.
  • Buttons: Log snapshot, Edit loads and Open layout.

The Ductulator

What it is for. A quick duct calculator that does not touch the design.

Where. Ductulator in the top bar. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Pick a tab: Size a duct, Check a size or Shapes.
  2. For Size a duct, type the cfm, pick Size by (Friction or Velocity), the friction rate in in. w.c. per 100 ft, the Material and the kind of Duct (supply trunk, supply branch, return trunk, return branch).

What you get. The exact round size and the size to use, with its velocity and friction, the next whole size, the velocity pressure, equivalent rectangles with their aspect ratio, and a table of round duct capacity at the friction rate.

Limits. The velocity limit shown with each kind of duct and the material factors are the app's starting values, not design values; set your own (see Ducts and duct sizing).

The usual order of work

  1. Design conditions: weather, envelope, ventilation and the code jurisdiction.
  2. Plans and the plan reader: bring in the architect's PDF.
  3. Layout: drawing the plan: floors, rooms, windows and doors.
  4. Rooms and loads: check each room's load.
  5. Equipment: compare equipment with the loads.
  6. Layout: equipment, registers and ducts: place the equipment and registers, draw the ducts.
  7. Ducts and duct sizing: read the sizes and the static.
  8. Price book and estimate, then Reports and letters.

For an existing home with no plans, start with the Home survey. For a commercial job, see also Commercial.
