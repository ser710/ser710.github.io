Plans and the plan reader

Contents

Two pages work with the architect's drawings. Plans & markup opens a PDF, sets its scale, marks it up and sends a sheet to the Layout as the underlay. Plan reader reads a whole plan set for the HVAC and mechanical callouts and can build rooms from a floor plan sheet. Both pages are in both editions.

Open a plan set in Plans & markup

What it is for. View the architect's sheets, set their scale and mark them up.

Where. The Plans & markup page. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Press Open (or Open files) and pick the PDF, PNG or JPG files. You can pick several at once. Blank sheet makes an empty sheet in an ARCH or ANSI size; Sample set opens a three-sheet HVAC set to practice on.
  2. Pick a sheet from Sheets.
  3. Set the scale: open Scale and pick a Preset, or use Calibrate on drawing, click two points on a dimension you know, type the Known length and press Set scale. Apply to all sheets sets every sheet at once.
  4. Use the markup tools on the sheet.

What you get. The sheets with their scale. The side tabs are Props (the selected markup's line, fill, text, layer, status and lock), Markups (the list of markups, with filters and Export CSV), Sheets, Layers, Scale and Find (text on the sheet).

Other tools.

  • Staple (Staple files…) joins several files into one set.
  • Header / footer… adds a header and footer to every sheet.
  • Label sheets from box reads the sheet number or title from a box you draw and names every sheet the same way.
  • Overlay compare lays one sheet over another to see what changed.
  • Export saves the set with its markups, with a choice of sheets and of markup summary pages.
  • The ready-made tool set has Supply duct, Return duct, Flex run, Supply register, Return grille, Revision cloud, Zone area and Field verify; + Save current keeps your own.

Limits. A scale you set is only as good as the dimension you clicked. Check it against a second dimension.

Send a sheet to the Layout as the underlay

What it is for. Draw the rooms and ducts over the architect's floor plan, at its scale.

Where. Plans & markup, the Use as Layout underlay button. From the Layout, the Underlay card has PDF via Markup, which opens this page. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Open the sheet and set its scale.
  2. Press Use as Layout underlay.

What you get. The sheet is drawn at its scale and placed under the plan on the floor you are on in the Layout. Move it there with Move underlay.

Limits. The underlay is a picture of the sheet. Rooms still have to be traced or built (see Layout: drawing the plan).

Snip part of a plan sheet

What it is for. Cut out part of a sheet as a picture: to paste into an email or another program, to save as a PNG file, or to paste back onto a sheet as a picture markup.

Where. Plans & markup, the Snip button. The same tool is in the markup tools as Snip (snipping tool). Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Open a sheet. Snip stays off until a sheet is open.
  2. Press Snip and drag a box over the part of the sheet you want.
  3. The Snip window opens with the picture. Pick one:
    • Copy image: puts the picture on the system clipboard, to paste into an email or another program.
    • Save PNG: saves the picture as a PNG file, named with "-snip" at the end.
    • Paste here: puts the picture on the sheet as a picture markup.

What you get. A picture of the part of the sheet inside the box.

Limits. It takes what is drawn on the sheet on screen. It does not read the sheet's text or sizes.

Mark up Aerion's own drawings

What it is for. Put clouds, callouts and stamps on the plan set, the permit report or the fabrication isometrics before they go out.

Where. The Reports page, card Mark up before it goes out: Plan set → Markup, Permit report → Markup (residential) and Fab isos → Markup.

Steps. Press the button. The finished PDF opens in Plans & markup. Mark it up, then save it with Export there.

Read a plan set with the plan reader

What it is for. Find every HVAC and mechanical callout in a plan set: equipment tags, air devices, duct sizes, airflow, dampers, piping, controls, static pressure, unit electrical, code and spec references, schedules and notes. On the other sheets it also picks out rated walls, ceiling heights, roof curbs and kitchen hoods.

Where. The Plan reader page. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Press Open plan set PDFs, or drop the PDFs on the page. Every discipline at once works best.
  2. Pick a sheet to see its callouts.

What you get. Each sheet's callouts, listed by kind. Tags on the plans are checked against the schedules and against the electrical and structural sheets. The PDFs stay in this browser.

Limits.

  • Text-based PDFs (printed from CAD with TrueType fonts) read best. A scanned sheet has no text to read: get the DWG or a reprint.
  • The reader goes by the words printed on the sheet. Check its list against the drawings.

How the plan reader picks out duct sizes

What it is for. A size like 14x8 is listed as a duct only when it reads as a duct size, so posts, beams, steel, windows and tile are left out.

Where. The Plan reader page. There is nothing to set: this is how the reader works.

A "number x number" is not taken as a duct when:

  • it has a third part, as steel and lumber with a length do (6x6x1/4, 4x12x10');
  • it follows a steel or lumber mark (HSS, TS, W, L, C, PL, LVL, PSL, GLB, DF, SYP, PT, DBL and the like);
  • a framing, structure or opening word is next to it (post, column, beam, header, joist, rafter, stud, ledger, truss, footing, pier, anchor, @ 16" O.C., window, door, skylight, access hatch, tile and the like) and no duct word is;
  • it is followed by cm, mm, m, ft or ', or a room, floor finish or house word is next to it (bedroom, kitchen, floor, ceramic, house, lot);
  • it is on a sheet that is not mechanical (architectural, structural, interiors) and no duct word is next to it.

A duct word next to the size always keeps it in: duct, supply, return, exhaust, SA, RA, EA, OA, CFM, plenum, trunk, branch, register, grille, diffuser, boot, transition, up and down. So "14x8 SA in joist bay" is read as a duct.

On a sheet with no sheet number, a bare size counts only when the sheet also has other HVAC callouts (two or more equipment tags, air devices, CFM or dampers).

Limits. A duct size with no duct word next to it, on a sheet that is not mechanical, is left out.

Build rooms from a floor plan sheet

What it is for. Make the project's rooms from the room labels and walls on a floor plan sheet, instead of tracing them.

Where. The Plan reader page. Open a plan set and pick a floor plan sheet. The Build rooms from this sheet card is with that sheet's callouts. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Pick the sheet.
  2. In Build rooms from this sheet, check Scale. The card shows the scale it read on the sheet.
  3. When the sheet has two or more floor titles, the card says so, for example "2 floors on this sheet: First floor, Second floor. Build rooms puts each on its own floor, lined up so they stack."
  4. Leave Build the whole sheet as one floor clear to build each plan on its own floor. Tick it to build the whole sheet as one floor, the plans side by side.
  5. Press Build rooms.

What you get.

  • A message that says what was built and how many rooms each floor really got, for example "2 floors from A-1: First floor 2 rooms, Second floor 2 rooms, lined up by the grid bubbles."
  • Each floor gets its own rooms and its own Layout underlay. The underlay is that floor's drawing only, without the page border, title block or site around it.
  • A floor title takes a project floor of the same name. The empty first floor of a new project is used only when no other title names it (it is renamed). Otherwise a new floor is made. No two titles on one sheet take the same floor.
  • The floors are listed bottom up (Basement, First floor, Second floor), as the Layout stacks them.
  • The floors are lined up by the grid bubbles and other short labels that repeat on every plan. Where there are none, they are lined up by the top left corner of each plan's walls.
  • Every floor gets the same drawing scale. Switch floors at the top of the Layout.

How the sheet is split. A floor title is large text such as FIRST FLOOR, SECOND FLOOR PLAN, MEZZANINE, LEVEL 2 or BASEMENT. Notes such as "ceramic floor" are not titles. Plans side by side are split along the emptiest line of drawing between their titles. Plans one above the other are cut at the first empty line under the upper plan's title. Each plan's area reaches out to the ends of its walls.

Limits.

  • Existing rooms are kept, and labels inside them are skipped.
  • A room named only REC ROOM is not built: it is not one of the room words the page knows.
  • Check the scale, that each floor got the right rooms, and how the floors line up, against the drawings.

Review a sheet with Aerion AI

What it is for. Get suggestions from an AI that can read pictures, on a drawn or scanned sheet: walls, doors, windows and room labels, and the visible dimensions, heights and HVAC callouts.

Where. The Plan reader page, Review sheet with AI. The same button is in Plan set to 3D on the BIM models page (see BIM models and plan set to 3D). Both editions. It needs Aerion AI set up (see Aerion AI).

Steps.

  1. Open the sheet.
  2. Press Review sheet with AI.
  3. Read the overlay on the sheet. Clear AI review takes it off.

What you get. On the Plan reader, an overlay of suggested walls, doors, windows and room labels, and a list of visible dimensions, heights and HVAC callouts, for review.

Limits.

  • Only a picture of that sheet goes to your configured AI, and only when you press the button.
  • On the Plan reader the overlay stays in the reader. It does not change the project's rooms or the loads.
  • A low-quality scan or a sheet with no known scale still needs hand tracing or a scale.

Read framing from a structural sheet

What it is for. Mark the roof and floor framing printed on a structural sheet, to use for duct routing and the framing view.

Where. The Plan reader page: open a structural sheet and press Read framing with AI. In Plan set to 3D on the BIM models page, open Read roof and floor framing to pick a source sheet. Both editions. It needs Aerion AI.

What you get. Aerion marks the hips, valleys, ridges, trusses, joists, rafters, beams, headers and bearing lines it can see, and reports the printed tags, pitch, spacing and drawing references when it can read them. The review is drawn on that source sheet for you to check.

Limits. The structural drawings govern. Member sizes, locations and roof shape are what the drawing shows or what the schematic assumes; Aerion does not size structural members or say where they may be drilled or cut. See Layout: 3D, sections, airflow and framing for the framing view.
