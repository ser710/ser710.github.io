Price book and estimate

Contents

The Materials page lists every part in the design and, with prices and labor turned on, builds the estimate. The Price book page holds the store prices the estimate can use. Both pages are in both editions.

The bill of materials

What it is for. List every part in the design, from the outdoor unit to the hanger strap.

Where. Materials, card Bill of materials. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Set Waste on duct, pipe and wire (%), the Refrigerant (R-454B, R-32, or R-410A for service) and Condensate pipe per unit (ft).
  2. Read the groups: Equipment, Air distribution, Duct and fittings, Refrigerant piping (residential), Controls and zoning, Electrical and Install and accessories.
  3. Press Preview takeoff, then Takeoff (PDF) or Takeoff (.csv).

What you get. Each line's item, size, quantity, unit and notes (for example which system it serves, or the measured footage plus waste). Change the design and the list follows. Hangers laid out on the Shop page replace the strap allowance. Return air pathways, end caps at trunk ends and the duct straight past them are listed. Loose duct pieces take their trade names.

Limits. The list is what the design shows. Check it against the job for anything not drawn.

Build the estimate

What it is for. Put prices and labor on the takeoff and get a job price.

Where. Materials: tick Show prices and labor (estimate). Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Tick Show prices and labor (estimate).
  2. Set Markup and Sales tax (%), the Labor rate ($/h), Overhead and Profit (%).
  3. Pick the Labor: Full labor: our crew does all of the installation, Half labor: the customer's crew does part of it, or A share of the labor hours.
  4. Put prices on the lines. Under Supplier price sheet, press Import price sheet and pick the price list your supply house sends (CSV or Excel): Aerion finds the description, part number and price columns and matches each line of the takeoff. Or press Load web retail starter prices. Or type a Unit price on a line.
  5. Check the Labor h / unit on each line.
  6. Add other costs under Job costs with Add a job cost (item, amount, labor).
  7. Press Save prices and labor for all projects to keep your prices and labor hours for every job.

What you get. The Estimate: Materials, Labor (with hours), Overhead, Profit, the Job price, and how many items are Unpriced. A line gives the job price with full labor and with half labor side by side, and says which the estimate is at.

How it is worked out. The card says it: labor is hours x the labor rate; overhead is a share of direct cost (materials with markup, labor, job costs); profit is a share of cost plus overhead; sales tax applies to materials only. Half labor changes only the hours charged.

Limits.

  • The labor hours start as example hours, and the card says how many lines use them. Replace them with your own in the Labor column, then save them for all projects.
  • Web retail prices are not your supplier's prices.
  • What the customer's crew does on a half labor job is for the Not included text of the proposal to say. The labor choice only prices it.

Price three options (Good / Better / Best)

Where. Materials, with the estimate on, Good / Better / Best. Both editions.

Steps. Type the Equipment cost for each option (blank uses the equipment in this design), then press Send these prices to the proposal.

What you get. Each option's job price. Everything else (ducts, labor, job costs, overhead, profit) is the same for every option. The proposal prints them at the labor level you chose (see Reports and letters).

Ask suppliers for quotes

Where. Materials, card Supplier quotes. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Type the Supplier and Contact (name, phone or email) and press Add supplier. With no name, the message asks for the supplier's name first.
  2. Set Quotes needed by.
  3. Send each supplier its request for quote, type or import the prices that come back, then use the lowest prices in the estimate.

Find a price in the price book

What it is for. Look up store prices for equipment, duct, fittings, air distribution, filters, controls, repair parts and more.

Where. The Price book page. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Type in Search (item, size or store).
  2. Narrow it with Category (Equipment, Duct and fittings, Air distribution, Filters, Controls and zoning, Electrical, Refrigerant piping, Install and accessories, Indoor air quality, Ventilation, Repair parts, Commercial refrigeration), Prices (All lines, Store prices, Earlier online lists, Estimated sizes) and Store.
  3. Use Previous and Next to page through. Price book (CSV) saves it.

What you get.

  • The matching lines with their unit, price, where from and category. The card at the top says how many lines there are, from which stores, and when each store's prices were read.
  • A size no store lists by name is estimated from the other prices and marked est.
  • Where two stores sell the same item, the estimate uses the lower price.
  • The stores include Home Depot, SupplyHouse, Ferguson Home, HVACDirect, The AC Outlet, ACDirect, Alpine Home Air and National Air Warehouse. Each store's products are lines with their model and a link to their page; scratch-and-dent and open-box items are marked.
  • The standard sizes the estimate prices (heat pump, condenser and air handler by ton, package unit by ton, gas furnace by input BTU, evaporator coil by ton, electric heat kit by kW, single-zone ductless by BTU) take each store's lowest price for a new unit of that size, and the lowest of all the stores is used.

Use the prices. Use these prices on Materials opens the Materials page and puts the web prices on the takeoff. The message says how many prices were loaded and that they are retail prices.

Limits. These are retail web prices on the dates shown, not your supplier's prices. Load your supplier's sheet on Materials for your own pricing.

Find repair parts for the project's units

What it is for. List repair parts that fit the units chosen in this project.

Where. Price book, card Repair parts for this project's units; Repair parts on an equipment line and Fits on a part line. Both editions.

What you get. For each unit chosen on the Equipment page (outdoor units, indoor units, furnaces, coils, rooftop units), the parts in three kinds:

  • Listed for this model: the part's page names the unit's model, its model with a revision suffix, or the series the part is made for.
  • Sized fit: compressors by capacity, refrigerant and phase, and TXV kits and expansion valves by tonnage and refrigerant, read from the part and unit names.
  • Same brand: the repair parts of the unit's brand family, opened as a Price book search.

Limits. A sized fit is a starting point: check the specifications. Match the model and serial number, and the part number, before ordering.

Commercial refrigeration units

The Price book's Commercial refrigeration category is the list the room editor picks from (see Rooms and loads). Each unit added to a room is a line under Equipment in the estimate, priced from the Price book; change the price there like any other line.
