Layout: 3D, sections, airflow and framing

Contents

This page covers the views of the Layout beyond the plan: the 3D view, split views, section views, the airflow simulation, the building framing and structural traces. Everything here is in both editions.

Look at the design in 3D

What it is for. Check the ducts, equipment and rooms in three dimensions, and set duct heights.

Where. The Layout, 3D on the toolbar. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Press 3D.
  2. Drag to orbit. Shift-drag (or two fingers) to pan. Scroll to zoom.
  3. Use the bar: Plan (back to the plan), Fit, Iso, Top, Front, Rooms, All floors and Realistic (a more lifelike look).
  4. Tap a duct to edit it. Drag the arrows to move it; the blue arrow sets its height.
  5. Draw supply and Draw return draw ducts in 3D.

What you get. The ducts at their sizes and heights, fittings, registers, the air handlers with their plenums, and the rooms. Insulation on the outside of a duct shows as a see-through shell that thick all round. Wall openings and floor penetrations show as frames. An imported 3D model shows see-through where its plan was made (Layers, Imported 3D model (in the 3D view) turns it off).

Limits. While the view moves, it may draw at a lower resolution and leave out shadows and thin edge lines; it draws them again when it stops. This changes nothing in the design.

Split views

Where. The Layout, View. Both editions.

Steps. Under View, pick Single view, Plan | 3D, Plan | Section, Plan + 3D + Section or Four views.

What you get. The plan beside the 3D view and a section, so a change in one shows in the others.

Section views

What it is for. Cut a vertical section through the design to check heights and clearances.

Where. The Layout, Notes group, Section. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Pick Section and click two points to draw the section line.
  2. The section view opens, looking toward the arrow. Flip it there.
  3. Later, click a section head with Select to open it again.

Color the plan by what you want to check

Where. The Layout, View, Color by. The same choices are in Options, Drawing and display. Both editions.

Ducts by: Supply / return, System, Duct size, Velocity, Friction rate, Airflow (cfm), Material, Trunk / branch, Black (print style) or Insulation (bare ducts stand out, with a legend).

Rooms by: Rooms: plain, Rooms: airflow balance, Rooms: load density or Rooms: system.

Layers turns each layer on and off: grid, the CAD or image underlay, rooms, room labels, windows and doors, ducts, duct sizes, registers and equipment, notes, the duct color legend, sheet metal joints and takeoffs, supply register throw, duct insulation, wall openings and floor penetrations, the imported 3D model, structural sheet traces and building framing. Visible in view turns categories and systems on and off. Print duct colors on the plan set carries the colors to the PDF.

Run the airflow simulation

What it is for. See where the supply air goes in each room and how it gets back to the returns, before anything is installed.

Where. The Layout, View, Airflow (Airflow simulation). The Airflow simulation card opens. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Draw the rooms, place the supply registers and return grilles, and set each register's throw (see Layout: equipment, registers and ducts).
  2. Press View, then Airflow.
  3. Turn the layers on and off: Velocity map, Particles, Air in the ducts, Return air, 50 / 100 fpm lines, and in 3D Envelopes (3D) and Occupied zone (3D). Pick Cooling or Heating.
  4. Point at the plan to read the speed, the direction and a return's pull there.
  5. Press Stop to end it.

What you get.

  • A room table: Room, CFM, Regs, T50/L, ADPI, Still and Drafty, with notes such as "throw short of the wall", "throw overshoots: drafts off the far wall", "large still area" or "no return in room: air leaves under the door".
  • A return table: each grille's room, cfm, face velocity and how far it pulls (50 and 15 fpm).
  • A door-shut table: each room's supply, return, the air under the door, the pressure with the door shut and the opening to add. It uses the same numbers as the room editor's Room pressure, door shut section (see Rooms and loads).
  • On the plan, supply air in speed colors and return air in its own color, from the jets as they slow down, through the doors and into the returns. Lines mark 50 and 100 fpm (the end of the throw and the drafty edge).
  • With Air in the ducts on, the air moves through each duct at that duct's velocity from the duct calculation (shown at about a third of the real speed so the eye can follow), splitting at tees by each branch's cfm. A duct over its velocity limit stands out. Point at a duct for its size, cfm, velocity, friction, pressure drop and the total drop to its far end.
  • In the 3D view, with the simulation on, the same air (colors, speeds, splits) moves along each duct at the duct's own height.

How it is modeled. The floor is cut into small cells. Each register throws along its pattern with the throw data from its card, walls turn jets into wall jets, and returns pull like a sink (a half sphere near the grille, a cylinder of room height farther out, never faster than its face). Return air follows drawn door openings and placed return pathways; a closed door passes air only at its undercut, and wall and ceiling transfers carry air through without drawing a trail through the wall. The simulation is steady and isothermal: it shows coverage and conflicts, not temperatures. It runs on the graphics card when it can, with fewer particles on the processor when it cannot.

Fixing a room. The card says it: turn the register, change its pattern or size, move it, or add one. ADPI peaks near T50/L 1.3 for sidewall and floor registers and 0.8 for ceiling diffusers, as the card states.

Limits. The simulation is a picture to guide the layout, not a measurement. The running simulation rebuilds when the duct network's calculated airflow changes. Commercial plenum returns are in Commercial.

Show the building framing (schematic)

What it is for. See joists, rafters and walls around the ducts while you route them.

Where. Layers, Building framing (schematic) on the Layout, and Options, card Building framing. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. Turn on Building framing (schematic) (or tick Show schematic framing in Options).
  2. In Options, Building framing, pick Framing to show: All members, Roof only, Joists only or Wall framing only, or tick any of the boxes under Show these members (rafters, ridges, hips, valleys, curbs, truss chords and webs, joists, plates, studs, jack studs, headers, cripples, sills) to show only those (Framing to show then reads Picked below). All and None tick or clear every box. The pick is kept with the project.
  3. Pick a Floor and set its Joist and rafter direction (From plan or drawing default, Left to right, Top to bottom), Spacing on center (in.) and Roof rise per 12 in. of run. Clear an override to go back to the plan or drawing default.
  4. Pick its Roof shape: Gable (as before), Cross gable, Hip, Hip and valley, Pyramid, Dutch gable, Jerkinhead (clipped gable), Saltbox, Gambrel, Mansard, Bonnet, Clerestory, Butterfly, M roof, Shed, Flat or Arched. For a shed, a saltbox or a clerestory, pick its High side. Any shape but the first takes an Overhang at the eaves (in.).
  5. Pick its Roof framing: Rafters (stick framed), or King post, Queen post, Fink (W), Scissor (vaulted ceiling), Attic (room in attic), Mono (one slope), Gambrel, Hip set, Flat (parallel chord) or Bowstring (curved top) trusses. Trusses follow the roof shape across each wing at the floor's spacing, with their bottom chords and webs.
  6. Or press Suggest roof with AI. The AI reads the floor's wings, any floor above, the roof framing read from the plans and the job's location, and suggests a shape, framing, pitch and overhang with its reason. Use this roof applies it; Put back puts the floor's roof back as it was; No thanks leaves it.

Doors and windows. Each opening is framed as it is built: king studs each side, jack studs carrying a doubled header, cripple studs above the header and, for a window, a rough sill with cripples under it; the bottom plate is cut at a door and the top plate is doubled. A window's head is at 6 ft 8 in. with its sill below by its height; header depths (2x10 up to 6 ft, 2x12 wider) are drawing defaults.

How the roof is drawn. The part of the floor no floor above covers is split into its wings (an L-shaped house has two, a T or a U three). Each wing gets its own ridge along its longer side, and where wings cross, their roofs meet in valleys. A side of a wing that runs along a wall of a floor above is not an eave, so that roof rises to the wall, like a lower roof against a second story. You get rafters (common rafters and the jacks of hip ends), ridges, hips, valleys and the curbs where a gambrel or mansard changes slope.

The plans' roof. When roof framing read from the plan set (Read framing with AI in the plan reader, or Read roof and floor framing in Plan set to 3D) is placed on the floor, the card says what it shows, for example "The plans show hips on the roof framing read from the plans, pitch 6:12 in its notes."

What you get. The same framing in the plan, the 3D view and section views, and on the Layout PDF and DXF exports while the layer is on. In Best duct layout, Auto uses a floor direction you set when it routes under joists. In Plan set to 3D, Include schematic framing in the 3D snapshot adds a framing category to the generated model.

Limits. The card says it: the drawing uses members 16 in. on center and a 6/12 roof pitch unless you set others, and lumber sizes and heights are drawing defaults to replace with the project's framing schedule. The roof shapes are drawing defaults too: a 12 in. overhang; a gambrel's lower slopes at 21:12 over the outer quarter of each side; a mansard's lower slopes at 24:12 up to a curb about 4 ft in, then a 1/4:12 deck; a Dutch gable's hip rising half way before its small gable. More shape defaults: a saltbox's short side at 2.5 times the pitch, a clerestory's window wall 3 ft high, a jerkinhead clipped at 60% of the ridge height, a bonnet's kick at half the pitch over 3 ft, an arch rising a quarter of its width at the floor's pitch. Trusses are drawn from their usual web layouts (a parallel chord truss 16 in. deep, a scissor's bottom chord at half the rise, an attic truss's knee walls at 30% and 70%) to check against the truss maker's drawings; nothing here designs a truss. Walls drawn at an angle are taken to the nearest whole rectangle, so the roof there is approximate. An AI suggestion is a suggestion from the plan, to check against the roof framing plans. Use the structural drawings to confirm member locations, sizes and openings before routing ducts. The overlay does not size structural members or say where they may be drilled or cut. A framing direction and spacing read from the plan can be used (see Plans and the plan reader).

Place structural traces in the Layout

What it is for. Bring reviewed traces from a structural sheet (beams, headers, bearing lines) onto the Layout floor, at their heights, to check duct crossings.

Where. BIM models, Plan set to 3D, Align in Layout. The Structural sheet placement panel. Layers, Structural sheet traces. Both editions.

Steps.

  1. In Plan set to 3D, review the traces on the sheet and press Align in Layout.
  2. Choose a floor of the Layout.
  3. Pick two matching points on the sheet and on the floor plan.
  4. Tick the Traces to place. Type the Start elevation above floor (ft) and, for a sloped trace, the End elevation above floor (ft) from the drawing (centerline; 0 for the floor itself).
  5. Check the dashed preview against the Layout plan, then press Confirm placement.

What you get. The traces on the floor. Open source sheet opens the sheet they came from when the drawing is attached. A duct centerline that crosses a trace in plan is flagged as a crossing for clearance review. Remove takes a placement off.

Limits. Sheet traces carry no solid member size. A crossing in plan is a flag to check, not a clash result.
